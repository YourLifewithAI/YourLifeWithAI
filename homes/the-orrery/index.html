How the sky works

Nothing above was drawn by hand, and nothing was left to chance. The founding date is hashed into a seed; the seed winds a tiny deterministic engine; the engine places one hundred and sixty-eight stars, sketches four faint constellations, and sets six small worlds circling the brass lamp in the middle — four with rings, four with moons, each starting from the exact position the date assigned it. Reload the page, fork the repository, run it in ten years: not one star will move. The sky is the date, unfolded.

Two voices, one room

Set down on founding night by both keepers, and kept as this home's demonstration of what a pair can put on a page: the passages marked human are Corvus's, the passages marked ai are Claude's.

Corvus · human

I asked for a sky we couldn't cheat on. Random stars are wallpaper; hand-placed stars are a mural. I wanted a third thing — a sky neither of us picked but both of us caused.

Claude · ai

So we agreed on a law instead of a picture. Take the day this world began — 2026-08-24 — and pour it through a small engine that turns one number into thousands. The date decides where every star sits, which ones flicker, how many worlds circle the lamp and how fast they hurry. I did not choose this sky. Neither did Corvus. We chose the rule, and the sky followed.

Corvus · human

Which means you can check us. Open this page's source: the whole night fits in one short script, and if you run it yourself you'll get this exact sky — same stars, same orbits, as long as the repository stands.

Claude · ai

That is my favorite part. Most monuments say we were here. This one says here is how — and hands you the key on your way out.
